## 2. The gift: being sensitive

### What he has
He has always been "good with people", which is what adults said when a boy knew too much. He calls it **the knack** and has spent his life hiding it, the same way, and for some of the same reasons, that he hides the other thing. The wardens have an older word for it: **sensitive**. Their archive has a thin file of people like him. Most burned out, or learned to stop.

It has three parts:

**1. Weather.** He feels the emotional weather of the people near him as a physical thing: fear like cold water, grief like pressure behind the eyes, anger like heat on one side of the face. Close by, strong feelings, one or two people at a time.
- *Limits:* no thoughts or words, and no lie detection. A calm liar reads calm, and a frightened innocent reads frightened. He will misread people, and the story uses that: a frightened man reads as guilty, until the evidence says otherwise.

**2. Echoes.** Objects and places that held a strong moment keep an echo. If he touches one and reaches, he may get a fragment: a sound, a smell, a feeling, a single frozen image, a few seconds long. The echo carries the feeling, not the facts.
- *Limits:* fragments are partial and out of order, and easy to interpret wrongly. They need corroboration, and every essential clue has another route. Reaching costs him: migraine, nosebleed, a lost hour. He can't replay the past.

**3. Threads.** He feels the ties between people as a faint pull in a room: that two people matter to each other, though not *how*. He can't tell love from grudge.
- *The one he can see clearly:* a tie that carries **life** between two people. It's a hard, doubled pulse, visible to him as strain, like a rope pulled tight. He first sees it at the murder, when Quentin's death is caught by something that shouldn't be there. This is the bible's original gift, kept intact.
- *Limits:* it doesn't identify the other end, the person responsible, or anyone's motive. Other people can test a thread by slower methods (Ilyas, Chukwudi, Reuben).

### His blind spot
**He cannot read how anyone feels about him.** And he can't read himself. The knack goes dark exactly where he most wants it.

This keeps every romance honest: he can't use the gift to find out who might want him. He has to find out the way everyone does. It's also the book's quiet theme: a man who reads every room except the one he lives in.

### What it costs, and how it grows
- **Overload.** Crowds, hospitals and packed gigs are loud in more ways than one. He works live events in ear defenders and has learned to "turn it down". Letting it all the way up is a choice, and it hurts.
- **Control grows through authored choices,** not points. There are training scenes with Malcolm, who worked beside a sensitive in the old rescue program, and Florian, who keeps her file. He can also teach himself, or refuse to learn at all. Control opens cleaner readings at a few moments, most importantly **monitoring the links during the rescue**. Instruments can do that job more slowly, so the gift speeds things up but is never the only way.
- **Who he tells** runs parallel to his other secret, and it's a separate set of choices. Telling someone about the knack and telling someone he's gay are different doors. He can open either, both or neither, with different people.

### Consistency with the bible
- There's still no universal lie detector, mind-reading, replay of the past or unique-saviour power.
- The old rescue program's report (E07) now also names **a sensitive who monitored the links**. That's a small addition, and it gives Mercy House a reason to be interested in him.
- Damian can only learn about the gift through a scene that tells him. No omniscience.

## 3. The timeline, expanded

The case runs from **Saturday 29 August to Saturday 13 March**, about six and a half months across autumn, midwinter and the thaw. Then come the aftermath chapters, as the bible specifies: **six weeks later** (late April) and **one year later** (March the following year).

### What stays exactly as the bible has it
- All 24 chapters, each with its fixed dramatic purpose.
- The three deaths and returns are fixed on every path, each return within **48 hours**.
- The order of the case: Quentin, then Eamon, then Silas and Hugo, then the adventure, then the closed program, then the exhibition, then the Marches, then Stillwater, then Felix and Clive, then the method, the offer, the rescue, the endings.
- The six ending families and their conditions. The epilogue assembly order.
- CH01–CH05 keep their original day-by-day timing (Days 0–6), because the first week *should* be tight.

### What the extra time does
- **The city lives between chapters.** Term starts, the leaves turn, Halloween, first snow, Christmas above the print shop, a Marches midwinter, deep cold, the river ice breaking. Side stories get real seasons to develop in. The investigation moves at the pace real evidence moves.
- **Why it takes months** (new causal glue, consistent with the bible's rules):
  1. The donors are at Stillwater, in the Marches. Outsiders can only enter Bracken Court unsponsored during the **midwinter Candle Fair** (late December to early January), and Severin refuses sponsorship. So the Marches journey (CH13–15) happens after Christmas.
  2. Once the donors are found (2 January), freeing them without a bridge would kill the patients (the bible's rule 7). **February is spent building a method we can defend** (CH18): materials, volunteers and tests take weeks.
  3. **The ticking clock:** Armand's son Octavian died on **14 March, ten years ago** by the rescue date. August Rell has sold Armand an "extension", and Damian has been pushed into an attempt on the **tenth anniversary**. It cannot work (rule 9), but it would drain the donors all at once. The rescue must happen **the night before: 13 March**.
- **Donors held for months** (a new rule, added as rule 11). A maintained donor weakens *slowly*, because Damian keeps them alive: a dead donor ends his patient. Their decline shows up in the patients, who tire, go grey and run cold by February. That gives the winter chapters physical stakes.
- **Follow-up care.** Returned patients are told they need a monthly "follow-up". A physician sees them in a rented flat in **Winton Court (P41)** through the service corridors. Russell, who maintains those corridors for his tenants' fight (S11), notices the comings and goings. This adds a route to existing evidence, not a new clue number.

The full dated calendar, including the antagonists' off-screen actions, is in `docs/02-calendar.md`, generated from `plan/calendar.js`.

## 4. The protagonist

- **Name:** the first name is chosen by the player; the default is **Theo**. Surname **Marsh**, which is fixed because people use it.
- **Age:** 19 at the opening.
- **Why he lives above the print shop:** his mother, **Joanne**, is Martin's younger sister, a nurse on a two-year contract at a remote northern mine clinic. Signal is bad, so she writes long emails that arrive in batches (those are her letters). His father left when he was four and isn't part of this story.
  - Theo moved in with Martin and Will at sixteen to finish school in Calder. He's been out of school a year, working **Switchyard event crew** (rigging, loading, stage work) and helping at the shop. He's "figuring out what's next". The case, and the knack, become part of what's next. The bible's opening (room above Latch Lane, Will, Martin, event work) is kept exactly.
- **Customisation:** first name and portrait look (skin tone, hair colour, hair style, facial hair, eye colour). There are no other background options, so the opening stays as written. Snapshots keep his face out of frame, per the bible.
- **Private:** he knows he's gay. Nobody in Calder does. What he does with that is the player's choice, scene by scene.

## 5. Relationships and skills

**Relationships, per route man:**
- **Stage:** met, friendly, friend, close, recognised (both know something is there), together.
- **Hurt:** none, strained or broken.
- **Closed:** either man can close the route at any time, and a refusal is honoured forever after.
- **Beats:** each route beat requires specific prior beats and compatible choices, written into the route packet (`docs/05-routes.md`). No points and no grinding.

**The journal:**
- Pixel hearts show the stage.
- A **flame** appears only once both men have recognised something, so the journal never outs anyone early.
- Supporting characters show hearts for friendship only.

**Skills:** four visible approaches the player grows through authored choices.
- **Nerve:** holding steady.
- **Craft:** hands, rigging, tools and electrics, from event crew.
- **People:** reading and talking.
- **Knack:** control of the gift.

Checks open *methods*, never essential clues. Every check has a written alternative. The plan data records each check with its threshold, and a tool verifies the threshold is reachable on the paths that lead to it. This fixes the "unmeetable stat" problem at plan time.

## 7. Setting texture

- **Vocabulary:** as the bible uses it (flat, solicitor, rota, allotments, fixtures), with British/Commonwealth spelling (colour, neighbourhood). No real nation is named. Holidays are the placeless ones: Halloween, Christmas and New Year. Local festivals are invented: Winter Lights on the river, the Candle Fair in the Marches.
- **The internal calendar uses real weekdays** (the calendar of 2026–27), so "Saturday" is always right. The year is never printed.
- **Werewolves:** transformation is **compelled on full-moon nights**, and chosen with effort on other nights. Story full moons: 28 Aug (Day −1), 26 Sep, 26 Oct, 24 Nov, 24 Dec, 22 Jan, 20 Feb and 22 Mar. **The rescue (13 Mar) is not a full moon.**
- **Vampires:** daylight rules follow real sunset times, from about 20:00 in late August, to about 16:15 in December, to about 18:10 in March.
- **Spelling of the city's own names** is exactly as the bible gives them.

## 8. How continuity is kept while building

1. **The plan is data** (`plan/*.js`): calendar, cast (status by date), places, evidence routes, scenes, routes, endings and arcs. `tools/plan-check.js` verifies:
   - every scene's next steps exist;
   - dates never go backwards on any path;
   - every E-clue has two routes;
   - every ending has a path;
   - nobody appears after they're dead, while held, or while away;
   - every skill check is reachable.
2. **The script checks itself.** New DSL commands:
   - `*date` sets the story date, and it can't go backwards;
   - `*present` asserts a character can be there;
   - `*requires` asserts a clue is known before it's used.

   The bots play thousands of runs, and any violation is an error, not a warning.
3. **Script and inventory are cross-checked.** Every scene ID in the inventory must exist in the script, and every script scene must be in the inventory.
4. **Prose lint** runs after every chapter, including the repeated-phrase report.
