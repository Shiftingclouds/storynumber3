# Calder: The Unquiet City — the story, for approval

*A read-through of the whole plan in plain prose. Spoilers throughout. ChatGPT's bible is the skeleton; this is the skeleton with the months put back in, the gift made sensible, and every chapter mapped to the scenes that play it.*

---

## The book in one breath

Theo Marsh (you choose his first name) is nineteen, lives above his uncle's print shop, rigs lights at a music venue, and has spent his life hiding two things: that he's gay, and that he can feel what people feel. One night in late August he watches a man die in the lane behind the venue, and feels something catch him as he goes. Three days later the dead man makes his coffee.

From the end of summer to the spring thaw, Theo follows that thread into a city he didn't know he lived in: wardens in an old hospital, vampires in a converted cinema, werewolf families in Eastbank, spell-workers on University Hill, and the Marches, another country through a door under a footbridge. Someone is killing people and bringing them back, each one kept alive by a stranger chained in a warehouse on the other side. By March he has to decide what it's worth to get them all home, and who he's become while trying.

---

## His gift: the knack

He calls it the knack. The wardens call it being **sensitive**. It has three parts:

- **Weather:** he feels the emotions of people near him as physical sensations.
- **Echoes:** he gets fragments of strong moments from objects and places.
- **Threads:** he feels the ties between people. He can see one kind clearly: a tie that carries *life* from one person to another, taut as a rope. That's ChatGPT's original gift, kept as one part of the whole.

**Limits:**

- It never reads thoughts or detects lies. A frightened innocent reads guilty, and the story uses that.
- Echoes can mislead, and every clue has a second route.
- It hurts to use, and it's loud in crowds, which is why he wears ear defenders at gigs.
- **He can't read how anyone feels about him, or read himself.** The knack goes dark exactly where he most wants it, so every romance is found out the ordinary way.

He can learn control (from Malcolm, a former warden, or from the notes of Ruth Carrow, the sensitive who worked on the old program), teach himself, or refuse. Who he tells about the knack is a separate choice from who he tells he's gay.

## The timeline

About seven months instead of ChatGPT's five weeks, with every one of its 24 chapter blocks kept in order. The first week stays day by day; after that, time moves the way a novel moves.

### Part One: Late Summer (29 August – early October)

**1. After the Last Set.** Saturday, above the print shop: Martin's overdue invoice, Will's game tomorrow, a batch of emails from Mum at her northern clinic. At Switchyard: Nolan on sound, and an apprentice electrician called Micah who had a "rough night" (it was the full moon). Quentin from Double Shift asks me for a plaster at the bar. At load-out, in the rear lane, Quentin dies, and the knack goes off like a struck bell. I hide and film, run at them, or run for help. Each gives different evidence and different risks.

**2. The Account I Give.** Sunday. What I tell Martin. What I do with it: the police, the venue, or only Nolan. Back in the lane by daylight, I can reach for an echo (white lilies, a calm voice counting down).

**3. Double Shift.** Delivering Martin's menus to a café in Northline, I find Quentin behind the counter, alive, with a rope running out of him through the wall. A young man in a formal collar, Ansel, asks the staff about a missing courier. I approach Quentin alone, bring Nolan, or wait six hours for the end of his shift.

**4. People Who Know.** I go back to the lane and meet the wardens (Adrian and Reuben), or Quentin's vampire brother Gideon comes to the print shop with Dominic, a singer who vanished from the scene a year ago. Either way I meet both circles, and everyone ends up at the Truss Road Diner with competing explanations.

**5. What the Body Keeps.** The hospital lab (Reuben, Ilyas, Rafi) or the restoration shop (Ellis and his father Chukwudi). Something outside Quentin is holding him up.

**6. Another Man Missing.** Two weeks on. The university term starts; Mum writes about a polar bear. Ansel's courier, Eamon, vanished the night before the murder on his way to a crossing. I find his route through the wardens' records or the night-bus drivers. His locker has an echo with the same calm voice.

**7. An Evening Already Promised.** Nolan's twentieth birthday, promised weeks ago; or Micah's family dinner in Eastbank; or Ellis's open-studio night. Keeping or breaking the promise matters. A cheerful depot mechanic called Hugo talks all night about a permanent job.

### Part Two: The Dark Half (mid-October – December)

**8. The Second Return.** A bakery apprentice, Silas, has a rope too. Hugo hasn't been seen since Monday. Quentin and Silas meet at the diner, two dead men pretending to be fine.

**9. Work Beneath the City.** Halloween, and a complete adventure: either something in the Northline tunnels that calls workers by name (the clue is a train announcement for a line cut seven years ago), or a haunted painted screen at the Okafors' that steals Isaac's shadow. Neither has anything to do with the killer.

**10. The Closed Program.** The wardens' archive or a recovery house on the ridge. Seven years ago Mercy House ran an emergency method that kept the dying alive on a donor's life. A donor was hurt, there was a hard 48-hour limit, and a sensitive named Ruth Carrow warned them. The knack gets a name. So does a former instructor Reuben loved: Damian Holt. And I learn that Commander Orrell buried the records.

**11. The Exhibition.** The Whitcomb Museum's winter opening, sponsored by grieving millionaire Armand Sorrell, whose son died ten years ago this March. Clive the ceramicist makes everyone laugh. Felix the student filmmaker caught a van at a charity gala labelled "Pump Nine". In case nine, a brass frame from the old program hums. Basil takes credit for Ellis's work.

**12. Before We Leave.** The full moon: a werewolf gathering at Quarry Lake, where a newcomer bolts for the cliffs. Or a frost-cracked roof at the Regent with an hour to sunrise. Or work: the Switchyard fundraiser, and a ghost at the Lantern Rooms trying to find a note his daughter sang flat. Quentin's monthly "follow-up" at Winton Court, where the calm voice is on the other side of the door. Then December: first snow, the Christmas rush, and Ansel's plan to cross into the Marches during the midwinter Candle Fair. I choose who comes.

### Part Three: Midwinter (Christmas – January)

**13. Bracken Court.** Christmas above the print shop, and a video call with Mum that freezes on her laughing. Then the door under the Iron Footbridge opens onto a market town with a candle in every window. I stay at the envoy's house or with the couriers, where Eamon's bag is still waiting.

**14. Passage Denied.** A lease dispute shuts the crossing over New Year. I get home by the court, by untangling Lucan's family debts, or along the Glass Road, where reflections show three threads running toward the docks. On quiet snowed-in days, the person I brought matters. On New Year's Eve, Ansel comes with no official reason at all.

**15. Stillwater.** The docks. I watch a boat unload Clive, who made the donors laugh, or find a witness inside. Through a high window: Eamon, Hugo and Clive in three beds, alive, the ropes running back to the city. We can't take them without killing the men on the other end.

**16. The Third Return.** Home. Felix is changed; the third rope runs to Clive. The three patients are matched to the three donors. The funeral-parlour lily on the van leads to a changed ledger entry, and the authorisation code is Damian's. Reuben sits down on a coffin trolley. I choose who I spend the rest of the week with.

**17. What I Ask of Him.** The deepest cold. I ask one person for something that matters: to volunteer, to lead, to trust, to stay. Or I tell Martin and Will the truth. Eight versions, one per man, plus a family version.

### Part Four: The Thaw (February – March)

**18. A Method We Can Defend.** February. Four ways to end it:

- **Distributed:** many volunteers each carry a little.
- **Interim:** one volunteer per patient.
- **Single-pair:** only one patient can be saved.
- **Extraction:** free the donors and let the patients die.

Materials, tests, a promotion review, a residents' vote. The patients make their own informed choices. I ask for volunteers, circle by circle. The keeper who smuggled people across the crossing is confronted, and so are Pump Nine and Damian's own notes.

**19. The Offer.** A cream card to Sorrell House, and three texts from the patients: each "follow-up" has moved to Saturday the 13th, eleven at night. Armand knows about the donors and wants one attempt for his son on the anniversary. I show him the 48-hour limit, and his hope breaks or hardens. Then I refuse, negotiate or arrange a supervised deal.

**20. Where I Stand.** The briefing: which plan we're ready for, and where I stand for the night. At Pump Nine with the patients, at Stillwater with the donors, or at the crossing keeping time. An hour at sunset with someone, or alone.

**21. The Links Between Us.** The night. Damian in a good plain coat, pleasant, asking excellent questions. Two things can go wrong that we saw coming. Then the choice of what we actually do with what we have.

**22. The Names We Can Say.** A week of consequences, and how much the city gets told.

### Afterward

**23. Six Weeks Later.** Spring. Mum's home. Will's trial. The side stories land, Benoît's showcase happens, and the relationship question is answered by both of us: together; together at a distance or with changed lives; over, after it mattered; friends; or my own life. And what I do next.

**24. One Year Later.** The assembled epilogue: where I am, every patient and donor by name, the relationship or the single life, two or three consequences I actually lived through, and a last image. The knack reads a room, and for the first time the room has me in it.

## The eight men

None of them is openly gay, and each discovers himself in his own way, as the bible describes. Relationships move through stages (met, friendly, friend, close, recognised, together) set by specific beats and choices, never points. Either man can close a route, and a no is honoured forever.

- **Adrian** (junior warden, 20) calls it professional concern. He needs me to disagree with him honestly.
- **Micah** (werewolf electrician, 21) calls it friendship. He needs someone to say no for him when he can't.
- **Ellis** (spell-worker, 20) calls it interest in a person worth knowing. He needs to be wanted on a bad day.
- **Dominic** (vampire, turned a year ago, 22) calls it gratitude. He needs to be asked, not managed.
- **Nolan** (best friend since sixteen, 19) calls it habit. He needs one direct, awkward conversation.
- **Ansel** (courier from the Marches, 21) calls it duty. He needs to come without a pretext.
- **Quentin** (the first returned, 22) calls it alliance. He needs room to make his own choices, and only if he lives.
- **Reuben** (warden medic, 23) calls it concern. He needs to be allowed to need something.

When pursued, every route reaches the relationship being recognised and then together, on more than one path. Every man has three possible romantic endings plus a friendship ending. Quentin also has a grief ending if he dies.

## The endings

Eight endings in six families, taken straight from the bible:

- **A. The Shared Return:** everyone lives, under accountable institutions.
- **B. A City of Witnesses:** everyone lives, and the communities keep the evidence themselves.
- **C. The Long Recovery:** everyone lives, slowly.
- **D. The Private Settlement:** everyone lives, and Armand keeps his name.
- **E. The Severed Bond:** the donors live and the patients die.
- **F. What We Could Save:** one patient lives, in three variants: Quentin, Silas or Felix.

No hidden morality score: preparation opens options, and the final choice is mine. Who answers for what (Damian, Armand, August) is fixed for each ending, so the prose can never contradict it.

## How it's being kept consistent

The plan is data, and `tools/plan-check.js` checks it: 262 scenes and 586 choices.

- Dates and times never go backwards.
- Nobody appears while dead or held.
- No vampire appears in daylight, and no werewolf on a full-moon night.
- Every evidence clue has its routes, and the essential facts are proven known on every path before the rescue briefing.
- Every skill check is reachable for a player who builds that skill.
- Every romance completes when pursued.
- All eight endings are reachable.
- Every epilogue assembles exactly one passage per slot, with the culprits matching the ending.

The same checks carry over into the real script as it's written, so problems get caught at the time, not afterwards.
