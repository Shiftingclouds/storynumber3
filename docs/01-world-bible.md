# PLAIN SIGHT — World Bible (draft for approval)

> *Everyone in this city is hiding something. Most of them are hiding it from themselves.*

Working title: **Plain Sight**. (Alternates: *Lookaway*, *The Hollow City*.)

A YA supernatural mystery with adult heart and heat. First person, present tense. One city, one year and a little more, a hundred places. A closeted nineteen-year-old comes to Calder to find out what happened to his missing brother, and learns to see: the hidden city, the people in it, and himself.

This document is the world. The storyboard, cast arcs, stat plan and continuity map come next, after you approve this.

---

## 1. The heart of it: the Veil is made of looking away

In Calder, supernatural communities have lived alongside humans for as long as there has been a city. Humans don't see them. Not because of a spell: because of a habit.

**The Veil is the human talent for not seeing what you've decided isn't there.** It's the same muscle you use to not notice the man sleeping in the bank doorway, the bruise on a friend's arm, the thing you felt at fourteen and filed away. The Hidden have simply learned to live inside that blind spot. A werewolf can run down Canal Street at midnight and a hundred people will remember a big dog. A vampire can own half the Hill and the tax records will list a holding company nobody ever looks into.

A few people can't look away. The Hidden call them **Wakers**. Most are children, and most grow out of it. Some don't.

**This is the theme and the engine.** The narrator is a boy who has spent his whole life perfecting the art of not-seeing — himself. The better he gets at looking honestly at who he is, the more of the city he can see. Self-honesty is literally his power. (It's private: seeing yourself doesn't mean telling anyone. Who he tells, and when, is a separate set of choices, and nothing forces him out.)

**The Hollow.** Things a city refuses to see don't vanish. They go somewhere. Under Calder, inside-out and grey, is **Hollow Calder**: every demolished street, every forgotten shop, every person everyone agreed not to remember, collected like lint in a pocket. You can walk into it, through **thin doors** that only open for those who see. Most people who go in don't come back out. Hollow Calder is growing.

## 2. The narrator

**Name:** chosen by the player (default: **Wes Calloway**). Nineteen. From **Aldermoor**, a small lake town three hours north: one high school, two churches, a hardware store his father runs, and everyone knowing everyone's business.

**Who he is:** a photography student, starting first year at Calder University on a scholarship he fought for, with his late grandfather's film camera. Funny, sharp, observant about everyone else and wilfully blind about himself. He is gay and entirely in the closet — so far in that he rarely lets himself think the word. He has a practised laugh, a practised shrug, and a list of reasons he's "just not into dating right now."

**Why he's really in Calder:** his older brother **Callum** came here to university and vanished three years ago, at nineteen. The police said he dropped out and drifted. The family stopped saying his name. Wes chose Calder University because it's the last place Callum was.

**His voice (sample):**

> The Red Line smells like wet pennies and somebody's curry, and I'm standing with my face an inch from a stranger's backpack, pretending I'm not reading the text on his phone. *where r u. bram's asking.* I don't know who Bram is. I'll know by Friday. I don't know that yet either.
>
> What I know is this: my brother rode this train. He stood in this exact car, maybe, holding this same pole, and he got off at Lantern Street at 1:40 in the morning on the eleventh of March, and nobody has seen him since.
>
> The doors open. I get off at Lantern Street.
>
> It's 1:40 in the morning. I didn't plan that. I'm pretty sure I didn't plan that.

**His camera:** the film sees what eyes won't. Photos taken on his grandfather's camera, developed in the university darkroom, show what the Veil hides: the eyes in the alley, the second shadow, the door in the wall that wasn't there. This is how he first finds out, and it's the in-world source of the **pixel snapshots**: every snapshot in the game is a photo he took.

## 3. The city of Calder

A river city of about 1.4 million, where the **Calder River** meets the great lake. Founded as a fur-trading fort in the 1700s, it boomed on mills and canals in the 1800s, built mansions in the 1880s, burned in 1891, rebuilt in stone, and has been quietly rotting and reinventing itself ever since. It feels like a real northeastern North American city — brick, stone, rust, ambition — without being any one of them.

**Seasons are serious here.** Humid summers, wildly beautiful autumns, winters that shut the lake down and freeze the canal, and a spring that comes all at once in May.

**Getting around:** the **Red Line** subway (and the sealed 1920s **Green Line** tunnels nobody uses — officially), the **Bellhaven Incline** funicular up the Hill, the cross-river **ferry**, night buses, bikes, and, if you know the right people, rooftops.

### The districts

Each district has its own sound, its own people, and its own Hidden.

**1. The University District ("the U")** — *where Wes lives his daytime life.*
Tree-lined streets around **Calder University**, founded 1821: stone quads, a brutalist 1960s library, students everywhere. Cheap cafés, used bookshops, bike racks, basement parties, the all-hours copy shop. At the edge of campus, on **Observatory Hill**, stands the old **Calder Observatory**, closed to the public "for restoration" since 1978.

**2. The Terraces** — *old residential streets; Wes's home.*
Steep streets of 1900s brick rowhouses with wooden porches and outside stairs, corner stores, a Portuguese church, a Polish one, old women on porches who see everything, kids playing street football, laundry lines across back alleys. Wes rents the attic room of a rowhouse on **Lark Street**.

**3. The Millworks** — *converted factories along the old canal; werewolf territory.*
Red-brick textile mills and a tannery along the **Delisle Canal**, now lofts, a brewery, climbing gym, artist studios, a weekend flea market, a boxing gym, and a warehouse club that's only open after midnight. The canal freezes in January and the whole district skates on it.

**4. Lowmarket and Lantern Street** — *the crowded district that never closes.*
The densest, loudest, most alive part of the city: noodle shops, pawnbrokers, 24-hour laundromats, karaoke bars, phone repair stalls, bakeries that open at 4 a.m. and bars that close at 4 a.m. and the hour between belongs to nobody. Under it, and inside it, and three doors down a particular alley: the **Night Market** of the Hidden.

**5. Bellhaven Hill ("the Hill")** — *old money; the vampire Houses.*
Mansions from the 1880s railway barons, gated gardens, private schools, a golf club, the funicular, the shuttered **Hotel Aurelian**, and views over the whole city. The streets are so quiet at night you can hear the sprinklers. Five old families own most of it. Four of them are vampires.

**6. Downtown ("the Spires")** — *glass, banks, and old stone underneath.*
Office towers, the stock exchange, hotels, the underground shopping concourse, the old **Merchants' Bank** building (now condos), the city hall with its clock tower, and the **Calder Courier** newspaper building.

**7. The Old Port** — *the waterfront.*
Cobbled streets, the fish market, the ferry terminal, tourist shops, shipping containers, the rusting **Pioneer Grain Elevator**, fishermen and dockworkers, and the Harbour Pack.

**8. Greymoor** — *the green: cemetery, arboretum and glasshouse.*
A vast Victorian garden cemetery grown into the city's arboretum, with a tangled old woods on its east side and the **Greymoor Conservatory**, a great glass palm house from 1874. People jog here, walk dogs, get married here, are buried here. The Gentry keep court here.

**9. Wicklow Island** — *the island in the river.*
An abandoned 1920s amusement park (**Wonderland Pier**: the wooden coaster, the carousel, the dance pavilion), a lighthouse, and a stone fort from the 1812 war. Reachable by ferry in summer and by walking on the ice in the worst of winter.

**10. Northside and the Cut** — *what the highway destroyed.*
In 1958 the city built the **Ridgeline Expressway** straight through the lower Terraces and demolished a whole neighbourhood, **Lower Terrace**, twelve streets of it. Northside is what's left: overpasses, empty lots, a few stubborn houses, a laundromat, a mural. Lower Terrace didn't disappear. It went into the Hollow. The Hidden know this; the city doesn't.

**11. Hollow Calder** — *the city under the city.*
Grey, silent, inside-out. Streets that were demolished still stand here, and shops that closed are still open with nobody in them. It's always the same dim hour. It's the "other dimension" of this story: where Callum is.

## 5. The Hidden: communities

The Hidden are not monsters or tourists. They're Calder's other citizens: families with feuds and weddings and funerals, businesses and debts, favourite bars and grudges older than the subway. They disagree with each other about almost everything, including the most important question: **how hidden do we want to be?**

### The Wardens (human hunters and keepers)
- **What they are:** human families with the Sight, who have kept the Veil and policed the Hidden since the city's founding. Trained, armed, disciplined, bound by oath. They're the Shadowhunters of this world, but human, and badly outnumbered.
- **Home:** Lantern House, in the old observatory: a training hall under the dome, an armoury, an archive of every Waker ever recorded.
- **Families:** the **Ashcombes** (leaders for four generations), the **Mercers**, the **Nakamuras**, the **Doyles**.
- **Tools:** lamps. Warden lamps burn with a flame the Hidden can't hide from, and warded iron.
- **Belief:** the Veil must hold at all costs; exposure would mean war.
- **Rivalries:** the Houses (old contempt) and the Packs (old blood). And more and more, their own young people, who ask why.
- **Favourite places:** the Observatory roof, Mulligan's (the young ones, in civilian clothes), St. Brigid's.

### The Houses (vampires)
- **What they are:** five old families of the Hill, bound by blood, money and centuries of etiquette. They don't hunt: they buy. They own the blood clinic, a bank and half of downtown's leases.
- **Houses:**
  - **Aldane** — the oldest and most powerful; its head is **Lady Seraphine Aldane**;
  - **Thorne** — roses and politics;
  - **Kessler** — money;
  - **Vasquez-Lowe** — the newest, turned in the 1920s, jazz-age and restless;
  - **Wren** — fallen, burned out in 1891.
- **Belief:** separation, absolutely. Humans are weather.
- **Rivalries:** each other, mostly. And the Wardens, whom they regard as armed servants who've forgotten their place.
- **Favourite places:** Hotel Aurelian, the Lookout, the back booth at the Blue Hour.

### The Packs (werewolves)
- **What they are:** working families. The wolves built the Millworks, worked the mills, drove the trucks, worked the docks, and they still do.
- **Packs:**
  - **the Millworks Pack** — the Kowalczyk family, led by **Dorota Kowalczyk**: the Tin Lantern diner, the Tannery, the boxing gym;
  - **the Harbour Pack** — the Byrne family, dockworkers and fishermen;
  - **lone wolves**, who belong to no one.
- **Belief:** mixed. The old ones want to be left alone. The young ones are tired of hiding who they are. (This is a thread.)
- **Rivalries:** Millworks vs Harbour, decades old, over territory and an old death. And the Wardens.
- **Favourite places:** the Tin Lantern, the canal on the full moon, the Anchor.

### The Guild (witches and warlocks)
- **What they are:** the Hidden's craftspeople and scholars. Magic is a trade, learned by apprenticeship: spell-work, wards, potions, bindings.
- **Institutions:**
  - **the Collegium** at Room 13, a secret faculty inside the university;
  - Guild shops across Lowmarket;
  - **the Registry**, which licenses and records practitioners.
- **Notable:** **Hollis & Vane**. **Augustine Vane** is 300 years old and has been "retiring" since 1911. His apprentice is **Felix Hollis**.
- **Belief:** pragmatic. They're the brokers between everyone, and they hate anyone who breaks the peace, because it's bad for business.
- **Rivalries:** the Registry against unlicensed hedge witches.

### The Gentry (the fae)
- **What they are:** old, strange, beautiful and bound by rules they cannot break: a promise is a promise, a gift demands a gift, a name has power.
- **Court:** the **Greenway Court**, which keeps court in Greymoor Conservatory after dark.
- **Changelings:** fae children raised by human families who don't know. Some grow up; some are called home.
- **Belief:** humans are fascinating and fragile. They don't care about the Veil the way everyone else does, because they've always had their own.
- **Rivalries:** everyone, lightly. Deals have long memories.

### The Rookery (the crow-kin)
- **What they are:** a sprawling family of shapeshifters who become crows. Couriers, lookouts, gossips and information brokers, running the city's rooftops.
- **Business:** **Blackwing Couriers**, bike and rooftop delivery. Anything, to anyone, by morning, and they'll know what it was.
- **Belief:** information should flow. They sell to everyone. That makes them trusted by no one and needed by all.
- **Favourite places:** rooftops, the clock tower, the Pioneer Grain Elevator.

### The Stoneborn (gargoyles)
- **What they are:** the city's oldest residents: carved guardians who woke up. Slow, patient, and they remember everything.
- **Home:** the Athenaeum roof, the clock tower, St. Brigid's, the old bank.
- **Belief:** they keep watch. They don't take sides; they're the city's memory.

### The Lingering (ghosts)
- **What they are:** the dead who stayed.
- **Institution:** **Night Radio 89.9**, a pirate station broadcasting from the abandoned transmitter on Wicklow Island. The ghost DJ plays requests from the dead. If you tune in at 3 a.m., you can hear them.
- **Belief:** they miss being seen more than anything.

### Others
A siren at the Blue Hour; a troll who runs the parking garage; a family of house-spirits in the Terraces; the thing that lives in the Green Line tunnels; and demons, rare and dangerous, summoned from outside the city by people who should know better.

### The Table (government)
- **What it is:** since the **Lamplight Compact of 1919**, the Hidden have been governed by the Table. It meets on the first Monday of each month in the old Merchants' Bank vault.
- **Seats:** one each for the Wardens, the Houses, the Packs, the Guild, the Gentry and the Rookery. The Stoneborn keep the minutes; the dead have no seat, and resent it.
- **What it decides:** territory, disputes, justice, and above all the Veil.

### The big political question: how hidden?
- **The Keepers:** hold the Veil exactly as it is. The Wardens' leadership, most of the Houses.
- **The Quiet:** thicken it. Less contact, harder lines. Some of the old Houses, and a frightened faction in every community.
- **The Threshold:** let humans in slowly, one family at a time. The young wolves, many witches, the Rookery.
- **The Unveiled:** tear it down. A small, angry underground.

Every character has an opinion, and the question mirrors the narrator's own: *how much do you let people see?*

## 6. The cast

### Wes's people
- **Callum Calloway** — his brother, 22, missing three years. Brilliant, reckless, a Waker, and the Wardens' secret informant. Lost somewhere in Hollow Calder.
- **Mom and Dad**, in Aldermoor — loving, stubborn and silent. His father has never said Callum's name since. They mean well. The closet was built partly with their help.
- **Mrs. Ines Barros** — his landlady, 78, a widow in black, and a former Warden who retired after 1958 and doesn't talk about it. She makes caldo verde and knows exactly what Wes is. Both things.
- **Maddie Chen** — his first friend at school: photography, loud, funny, with a crush on her female TA that she won't admit. Her own discovery.
- **Sam Okafor** — his first-week roommate and then best friend: pre-med, easy, kind, and a stubbornly regular human who takes forever to see anything. Possibly one of the awakenings; possibly just the best friend Wes ever has.

### The men who might awaken (none of them openly gay; all of it depends on you)
- **Julian Ashcombe** — Warden, 21, heir to Lantern House. Disciplined, golden, arrogant, brave, and the loneliest person in the city. He thinks Wes is a liability who'll get people killed. His awakening means breaking everything he was built to be.
- **Bram Kowalczyk** — wolf, 22, the second son of the Millworks Pack. He works the diner grill and fights in the ring. Cocky, loud, a flirt with every girl in the Millworks, and Wes's rival from their first shove. His is the furious *I hate that it's you* awakening.
- **Casimir Aldane** — vampire, looks twenty, turned in 1889. Heir of House Aldane, a bored, exquisite aristocrat who has never once let himself want anything. His awakening is a hundred and thirty years late and terrifies him.
- **Felix Hollis** — warlock apprentice, 23. Chaotic, charming, flirts with everyone of every species, and it's all a performance. He's the one who suspects most about himself, which is why he jokes hardest.
- **Jonah Rook** — crow-kin courier, 20. Fast-talking, funny, knows every secret in the city except his own.
- **Linden** — a changeling, 19. A first-year who doesn't know yet that he's fae. The Court is coming to call him home, and he'll have to choose.
- **Sam Okafor** — as above.

### The women and the elders
- **Harriet Ashcombe** — High Warden, Julian's mother. Iron, grief, and a secret.
- **Tamsin Ashcombe** — Julian's sister, 17, a Warden trainee who wants out. She's working out that she doesn't want what everyone wants for her either.
- **Dorota Kowalczyk** — alpha of the Millworks Pack. She runs the diner, the family and the neighbourhood with a spatula and an iron will.
- **Lady Seraphine Aldane** — head of House Aldane, Casimir's maker. Beautiful, very old, very kind until she isn't.
- **Augustine Vane** — the ancient warlock. He knows a great deal and says little of it for free.
- **The Lady of the Greenway** — queen of the Gentry court, who never tells a lie.
- **Detective Ruth Adebayo** — a human cop with a cold-case file of eleven missing young people. She isn't a Waker, but she's trying very hard to become one.
- **Old Grey** — the gargoyle on the Athenaeum roof, Wes's first Hidden friend, who speaks one sentence a week.
- **DJ Nightingale** — the ghost DJ of Night Radio 89.9, dead since 1962 and still taking requests.
- **Aunt Rosalind Wren** — the last of House Wren. Everyone says she burned in 1891.

## 7. The mystery

### What Wes knows at the start
Callum vanished at Lantern Street at 1:40 a.m. on 11 March, three years ago.

### What Wes can discover, over the year
1. **Callum was a Waker,** working secretly for the Wardens as an informant in the Night Market.
2. **Callum isn't the only one.** Eleven young Wakers have vanished in eight years. Detective Adebayo's cold-case file and the Wardens' archive list the same names, and the two records disagree.
3. **Every community blames another.** The Wardens blame the Night Market; the wolves blame the Houses; the Houses blame the Wardens. The Table is close to war over it.
4. **The missing are in Hollow Calder,** and the Hollow is growing: a building a month now, where it used to be one a decade.
5. **Someone is feeding the Hollow on purpose.** The Hollow takes what a city refuses to see, and somebody has learned to *make* the city refuse to see a person.
6. **The answer** (planned; the full solution comes in the storyboard):
   - The Wardens have a secret practice called **the Quieting**. It was meant to hide Wakers who were in danger by making the world forget them.
   - It has been used, for eight years, to dispose of Wakers who could expose the Hidden.
   - Harriet Ashcombe authorised it. Callum found out, so he was Quieted.
   - Julian doesn't know.
   - And behind Harriet is someone who has been thickening the Veil since 1891, for her own reasons: the last of House Wren.

### How the mystery plays
- **Clues:** you photograph them, find them, and are told them.
- **Your chart:** a map of Calder you draw on as you learn, with its districts, the thin doors, and the spreading grey of the Hollow.
- **Suspects:** you work out the truth by connecting clues, and you can accuse the wrong people. That costs you allies, and sometimes it costs more.
- **What you can conclude:** a player can finish the year knowing everything, some of it, or the wrong thing. The endings follow from it.

## 8. The year

The story runs from late August to the following autumn: a full year of seasons, with the time between big events allowed to move. Some chapters cover a night; some cover a month. Aldermoor is visited at the holidays.

**Autumn: Seeing**
- Moving in; orientation week; the first photos that show too much; the first night at Lantern Street.
- Meeting the Hidden one by one: the diner, the bookshop, the observatory roof.
- The Hunter's Moon on the canal.
- Thanksgiving at home: the first time Aldermoor feels small.
- **The Thin Night** (31 October), when every door in the city opens a crack. The first trip into the Hollow.
- November rain, midterms, and the first Table meeting.

**Winter: Hiding**
- The first snow on the Quad.
- The **Solstice Market**, when the Night Market spills into the streets.
- Exams; Christmas in Aldermoor, when the closet is at its tightest; New Year's Eve at the Houses' ball in the Hotel Aurelian.
- January dark; the canal frozen; skating with the pack.
- February: the Table breaks, and war starts in the Millworks.

**Spring: Breaking**
- March: the anniversary of 11 March, and the thaw.
- Spring break: a road trip north, or not.
- **May Eve** in Greymoor, when the Court comes to claim Linden.
- The lilacs; exams; the truth about the Quieting.

**Summer: Showing**

## 9. Game features

**Kept from Nuit Blanche:**
- the journal, with people, pixel hearts, flames and memories;
- the codex;
- chapter and ending cards;
- checkpoints and New Game+;
- the story map at the end, with a Reveal all paths button;
- the fade-to-black setting;
- offline first, with Claude as an optional narrator;
- the Windows exe and the single-file build.

**New and approved:**
- **Pixel snapshots:** Wes's photos, developed in the darkroom and kept in an album. A roll of film is limited, so what you choose to photograph matters: film is a resource, and sometimes the photo is the clue.
- **Letters:** from Mom in Aldermoor, from Mrs. Barros when you're away, from the Houses on cream card stock, from the Court on leaves, and from Callum, found hidden in the places he used to go. You write back.
- **Sight:** the more honest Wes is with himself, the more of the hidden city he can see. It's private, and separate from who he tells.
- **Your chart:** the map of Calder you draw on as you go.
- **Better portraits:** the rebuilt system (real eyes, jaw-following beards, polished hair), with a sample sheet shown to you before any writing.
- **Tools:** prose lint and the continuity checker from day one.

**Voice:** first person, present tense, throughout.
