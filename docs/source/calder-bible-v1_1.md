## 1. The agreed creative brief

The game is an ensemble urban fantasy, inspired by the pleasures of a large supernatural city: hidden communities, dangerous encounters, secret histories, friendship, attraction, and discoveries that change one's understanding of familiar places. Its prose is first-person present. The player character is a gay man. The tone has YA immediacy, warmth, humor, desire, and momentum, with room for adult moral and emotional complexity. The initial protagonist age is provisionally nineteen; all eight proposed romance characters are nineteen or older.

Calder is the principal setting. Its residents have homes, shifts, studies, money problems, loyalties, celebrations, and disagreements. The main investigation travels through those lives. A novel can stay within one city while becoming geographically, socially, and emotionally extensive. Time passes according to what events require. There is no alternating close-scene/summary mode, compulsory seasonal structure, or requirement to narrate every journey, meal, or day.

The narrator remains embodied in one viewpoint. The wider world is expressed through prewritten actions, messages, absences, changed circumstances, and encounters. Planning can know an antagonist's actions; the prose cannot report those actions as facts until the narrator has a route to that knowledge. Time advances at authored transitions. There are no simulated background turns, improvised errands, unrestricted travel commands, or automatically invented NPC actions in the canonical story.

## 2. The city before the plot

Calder is a fictional contemporary inland city of roughly 700,000, with surrounding commuter towns and wooded ridge country. This is invented geography, not a claim about an actual municipality. The River Calder runs south through its developed center. The Old Ward and Market Crescent occupy the west bank; Eastbank lies opposite. Industry spread downstream into Foundry Reach. University Hill rises northwest. Railways and bus depots cut through Northline on the northeast side. Briar Heights occupies affluent western uplands. Southmere extends southwest along a disused canal. North Ridge is reachable by regional bus or car.

The city has several centers. University life does not stop when a nightclub closes. Factory shifts begin when students go home. Wealthy residents cross neighborhoods for work, worship, treatment, or family. Supernatural communities are concentrated in some places but are never perfectly segregated. Human relatives, partners, tenants, coworkers, and customers connect them.

Most people accept ordinary explanations for extraordinary incidents because those explanations usually fit the visible evidence. Specific concealments can alter attention or obscure an entrance; there is no universal spell that makes every citizen incapable of seeing a monster. Phones work. Cameras record things. Evidence can be incomplete, contested, deliberately destroyed, or difficult to interpret. The story must account for convincing footage rather than dismiss technology whenever it becomes inconvenient.

### Everyday calendar

Classes, paydays, sports fixtures, family meals, markets, rehearsals, leases, appointments, and shift work create ordinary time. Rain changes travel and outdoor plans. Winter darkness changes vampires' schedules. A neighborhood celebration can precede a frightening discovery without being secretly organized by the murderer. Birthdays and anniversaries belong to people rather than functioning as automatic plot deadlines.

## 4. Supernatural life and limits

### People and capacities

**Wardens** are trained human practitioners who maintain protective works, investigate predation, and respond to breaches. Training provides techniques and specialized tools; it does not make them inherently more virtuous or important than other people. Their authority depends on cooperation as well as force. New recruits still need sleep, first aid, transport, and someone to cover their shifts.

**Werewolves** live in extended communities that provide help with transformations and the risks of heightened senses. They remain individuals. Family loyalty can be comforting, useful, intrusive, or coercive. Pack positions are social offices, not biological commands that erase consent. There is no automatic mate bond, sexual destiny, or personality change that excuses cruelty.

**Vampires** are embodied people sustained by blood. New vampires need practical assistance and must reorganize work and family life. Sunlight is dangerous, so schedules matter. Feeding arrangements create genuine ethical and economic questions. A vampire is not automatically a seducer, criminal, aristocrat, or expert in sexuality. Turning and the return phenomenon are different processes with different evidence.

**Spell-workers** learn methods with specific materials, procedures, and limits. People have uneven aptitudes. A talented restorer may be poor at healing or protection. Magic cannot answer any question merely because a character is described as powerful. Repairs, wards, binding, sensing, and transformation are distinct skills.

**The Marches' inhabitants**, commonly called fae by Calder's residents, have their own towns and institutions. Their affinity for thresholds and binding promises is a cultural and supernatural specialty, not a license for arbitrary word games. Enforceable bargains require recognized participants, understood subject matter, and a binding act. Casual speech does not accidentally sell a soul. Time passes at the same rate on both sides of a crossing. From Bracken Court to Stillwater Docks, allow roughly two hours on the local road under ordinary conditions; the Glass Road and Boundary Orchard require their own journeys. A crossing delivers people to a fixed place, not directly to their intended destination.

**Ghosts** are partial survivals with uneven memory, access, and persistence. A medium cannot summon any dead person on demand or assume a ghost's interpretation is correct. **Demons** are predatory nonhuman beings with intelligible needs and differing capacities; they are not a catch-all explanation for every unsolved incident. Most supernatural residents want protection from them.

### What keeps a mystery investigable

There is no universal lie detector, unrestricted mind-reading, convenient amnesia, limitless time travel, or all-purpose resurrection. Different procedures leave distinguishable physical and magical traces. Evidence has provenance. Witnesses know what they witnessed, plus whatever they learned afterward. Glamours affect perception in specified ways and can fail under specified conditions; they cannot be rewritten to erase the clue that would solve a difficult scene.

The protagonist's unusual ability is provisionally **perceiving an active bond between a person and an external source of vitality**. He first experiences it as a brief double pulse or visible tension during the opening death. It does not identify a donor, diagnose motives, replay the past, compel honesty, or make him a unique savior. Other people can test the bond by slower methods. Its origin need not be a hidden royal bloodline. This ability is a proposed design choice, not a player personality trait.

### C01 — Adrian Keene, 20: junior warden; romance possibility

Adrian wants a field appointment earned without his brother's name attached. He is compact, carefully groomed, and much more animated with old friends than with strangers. He keeps replacing the elastic in a jacket everyone tells him to throw away. Competence has been his safest route to affection since childhood; being useful feels less embarrassing than asking to be wanted.

He is patient with frightened civilians and impatient with colleagues who omit details. Under pressure he mistakes confidence in a procedure for confidence in its outcome. His private shame concerns an inaccurate training report that protected Emmett, not a hidden evil allegiance. He must eventually choose whether loyalty can include admitting what actually happened.

**People and places:** brother Victor C17; trainee Emmett C21; professional rival Darius C20; family ally Orrell C18; P01/P38. Adrian's promotion and the report have prewritten outcomes, including a background outcome used when the player does not foreground his arc.

**Voice:** complete, practical sentences; asks precise questions; jokes become surprisingly silly when he feels unobserved. Bad news makes him over-explain the sequence of events.

**Private attraction:** he has categorized every unusually important male friendship as admiration or competition. Attraction first appears as unwanted awareness of proximity and frustration when his concern exceeds his professional role. Progress requires seeing the protagonist handle a disagreement honestly, then admitting his own investment. Affection cannot excuse controlling behavior. He remains a person who likes planning even after he learns to ask rather than direct.

### C02 — Micah Serrano, 21: werewolf; apprentice electrician; romance possibility

Micah wants enough reliable work to rent his own place. He is broad through the chest, quick to laugh, and habitually arrives carrying something another person forgot. He can occupy a crowded kitchen easily, yet becomes evasive when asked what he wants for himself. His family made mutual help a source of pride; he has learned to treat declining a request as proof of selfishness.

He is generous about ordinary mistakes and less forgiving when someone treats Eastbank as dangerous or unsophisticated. He can make commitments he cannot meet, then resent the people who believed him. An outside apprenticeship offer threatens the household's assumptions about his availability.

**People and places:** father Ernesto C25; brother Leandro C26; cousin Tomas C27; coworker Pavel C28; old acquaintance Nolan C05; P07/P08/P27/P49. His work offer and family disagreement proceed without romance.

**Voice:** lively, interrupted by examples and remembered incidents. He becomes blunt rather than louder when truly angry. Practical affection arrives as inclusion: an invitation, a saved seat, an assumption that someone is coming too.

**Private attraction:** his past attraction to women was real. The possibility of desiring a man has not become part of his account of himself. A developing bond surprises him through how persistently he wants one particular person's company. His exploration is personal and sometimes awkward; he cannot explain everyone else's sexuality. A strong route includes occasions when the protagonist respects a boundary Micah has difficulty asserting.

### C03 — Ellis Okafor, 20: spell-worker and conservation student; romance possibility

Ellis wants a competitive placement outside Calder and worries that his departure will be interpreted as abandoning his father. Tall and fine-boned, he dresses with care on very little money and repairs the same bag repeatedly. He is skilled at making a difficult visit go smoothly. That social ability has persuaded people that he needs less reassurance than he does.

He loves technical problems because they allow him to revise a mistake without explaining what it meant emotionally. He can curate another person's impression of him until the relationship becomes exhausting. At home he is much less elegant: irritable about chores, protective of his materials, and easily drawn into ridiculous arguments with Isaac.

**People and places:** father Chukwudi C37; brother Isaac C38; colleague Caspar C40; classmate Felix C52; demanding tutor Basil C41; P19/P20/P21/P22. His placement and a disputed restoration generate a complete nonromantic arc.

**Voice:** precise about things he knows, associative when excited, evasively humorous when a conversation gets personal. His answers become noticeably shorter when he is disappointed.

**Private attraction:** he has noticed men privately and repeatedly postponed deciding what that means. There is no male ex or established queer social circle. He can discuss beauty confidently and still be unable to say that he wants to be touched by someone specific. ### Wider population and use of the roster

During development, writers can add a recurring person when an outlined scene needs a continuing relationship or remembered action. Every addition requires a profile, continuity links, scene assignments, and any necessary art before publication. The fifty-eight current entries establish the planned ensemble. Canonical runtime narration cannot generate new named characters, relatives, factions, or romance candidates. Background residents can receive authored roles during development while the current male-led focus and no-openly-gay constraint remain in force.

New names should be checked against existing sound patterns and relationships. Avoid introducing a cluster of similar names in the same scene. The roster deliberately includes different ages, work, resources, and levels of supernatural knowledge. Introduce those differences through behavior and obligations rather than a narrated census.

### Supernatural adventure also has its own life

The city should offer rooftop pursuit, training, frightening encounters, discovery, and shared danger as well as work and domestic life. The following six incident premises feed specific prewritten chapter branches, assigned in Section 9. Each selected incident needs a fixed cause, evidence, scene sequence, and resolution. They are not an endlessly generated quest pool.

**A predator in Northline's service tunnels.** A demon that imitates brief sounds has begun drawing maintenance workers away from lit areas. Owen's account, Pavel's knowledge of the site, Nolan's familiarity with recorded sound, and Adrian's field skills provide different ways into a contained investigation. The creature repeats a station announcement that has changed, supplying a fair clue to its imitation. A pursuit can end in a difficult rescue beneath passing trains. Solving this case demonstrates competence and changes workplace relationships. Its origin is independent of Damian.

**An inheritance brought to the restoration shop.** An ordinary family delivers a painted screen whose binding has been damaged during a move. Something confined within it is trying to borrow a living silhouette. Ellis needs time to understand the work while others protect the clients and contain an increasingly mobile threat. The previous owner may have acted cruelly or protectively; investigating provenance supplies a small complete mystery with an actual answer. The object is finite and its behavior observable. It cannot manufacture any effect the writer wants.

**A night outside Eastbank.** A supervised transformation gathering in North Ridge introduces the physical freedom, vulnerability, companionship, and practical work of werewolf life. The protagonist sees friends in a context they know better than he does. A disturbance may come from a frightened newcomer, a human trespasser, or an unrelated creature; choose one cause and develop its consequences. There is room for exhilaration, laughter, fatigue, and unexpected tenderness. Transformation never dictates whom someone desires.

**The Regent before dawn.** Building damage threatens the light protection in one residential wing. Residents must cooperate to move people, protect their human staff, repair the structure, and decide what can be left behind. Dominic knows whose room is where; Milo knows the projection and maintenance passages; Rafi knows who needs help. This is a tense community sequence whose stakes come from already knowing the residents. It can also expose the unequal treatment Abel expects from others.

**A crossing closed during a visit.** Ansel and the protagonist are in Bracken Court when a political dispute halts passage. Travel becomes an inhabited interlude: lodging, an unfamiliar town, Lucan's household, a local celebration, and work needed to secure another approved route. The dispute has a stated cause and people with authority to resolve it. Quiet days can acquire intimacy alongside a trip into unsettled border country; the closure is not a device to force sex or remove the player's boundaries.

**A ghost with an ordinary unfinished obligation.** Jonah encounters a partial haunting near the Lantern Rooms. Its surviving repetitions concern a task whose purpose has been misread. Investigation involves acquaintances, a place that changed use, and one surviving object. Resolving it can bring grief and relief without delivering a murder clue or a cosmic revelation. The ghost cannot answer unrelated questions about the central case.

### What the main characters can actually do in danger

Adrian is trained in protective lines and close fighting but needs a defensible position and cannot shield everyone at once. Micah has enhanced senses, strength, and mobility, with fatigue and sensory overload as practical limits. Ellis can inspect, repair, and improvise known bindings with appropriate materials; he cannot invent a new discipline in a crisis. Dominic's speed and recovery help him act, while sunlight and feeding needs change what risks are available. Ansel understands thresholds and negotiated passage; location and permissions still matter. Reuben stabilizes supernatural injuries and coordinates care. Action should respect positions, exits, light, noise, bystanders, injuries, and the aftermath. Shared danger can reveal character; ordinary scenes afterward let that revelation become a relationship. A thrilling rescue and a later argument about the risks can both belong in the same novel.

## 8. Author-only mystery solution and evidence

**Detailed case spoilers follow; the character profiles above also contain private author knowledge.** This section is intended for Trent and Claude as designers. Keep it separate from any later player-facing context. It is a proposed solution for this draft, not an obligation to retain the murder premise if a stronger story emerges. The cast and city should remain usable if the central case changes.

### 8.1 What actually happens

Damian Holt C56 has revived a closed research method that preserves a recently dead person's remaining connection to life. The method is real. Its original purpose was emergency rescue after supernatural injury. It was abandoned following an injury to a donor, weak safeguards, and institutional concealment of responsibility.

The current operation uses one captive living donor to sustain each returned person. Quentin C07 is linked to Eamon C49; Silas C51 to Hugo C57; Felix C52 to Clive C58. The returned people are themselves, not impostors or demons. The donor survives in a suppressed state and weakens as the link is maintained. People are held at Stillwater Docks P56 in the Marches; the linking apparatus and preparation work are at Pump Nine P16. Physical transit records connect those sites through known crossings. There is no instantaneous reach to any conveniently selected stranger. Hugo and Clive should be encountered or independently established before their abductions, and their people begin looking for them when they miss obligations.

Damian arranges deaths because a predictable time, a prepared tether, and a recoverable body make his results more repeatable. That is his chosen justification for murder, not an objective requirement that research must be conducted this way. Quentin is the first current success. Silas is the second. Felix is killed after noticing the operation and then used as a further controlled case. The ring regards his dependence as leverage that will silence him. This assumption can fail because Felix remains an agent with friends and judgment.

Armand Sorrell C44 supplies money and political shelter. He hopes to restore his son Octavian, dead for nine years. August Rell C39 knows that this is impossible under the method's actual limits, but continues selling the possibility of an extension. Damian initially considers the money a means to useful research; he comes to regard any threat to the project as a threat to all the lives it might theoretically save. Both men exploit Armand's hope while benefiting from his willingness to look away. Once Armand learns about the captive donors, his continued support is a knowing choice.

Soren C55 arranges property access and hides the true use of Pump Nine. Oswin C50 rents the docks space. Harlan C46 facilitates unauthorized passage. Their knowledge is unequal and changes over time. Simeon C19 handles a body through an old professional arrangement and conceals irregularities. Orrell C18 suppresses details of the old program because he fears institutional collapse, but has not commissioned the present crimes.

### 8.2 Rules that must be fixed before writing clues

1. **A recent connection is required.** The return must be initiated within forty-eight hours of actual death. After that, an object may preserve impressions, but cannot restore its former owner. Nothing discovered in the climax extends the window to nine years.
2. **Preparation helps but does not grant immortality.** A prepared protective token can preserve a usable tether briefly after death. A token is not a soul, a replacement body, or permission for remote control of a living person.
3. **The returned individual is the original person.** Memory may be incomplete around unconsciousness, injury, and movement, but there is no general amnesia power to hide inconvenient facts. Identity remains a philosophical and emotional concern without being an arbitrary twist.
4. **The body must be recoverable.** The process repairs a limited range of injury using borrowed vitality. It cannot build any body from dust, recreate someone long dead, or cure every condition.
5. **A link has a traceable source and material anchors.** It can be detected, tested, obstructed, and transferred only under defined conditions. The protagonist's perception speeds a first recognition; others can investigate it independently.
6. **The single-donor model is a choice made for control.** A distributed voluntary support method exists in incomplete earlier work. Multiple informed adult participants can provide smaller contributions through a properly rebuilt arrangement. It requires genuine coordination, recovery time, materials, and technical work. It cannot be improvised solely because a romance score is high.
7. **Recovery is possible but takes time.** A successfully returned person can eventually sustain life without the artificial bridge if the initial injuries are within the process's limits and the bridge is gradually reduced. Damian keeps patients dependent longer to obtain data and retain leverage. Freeing a captive must therefore be coordinated; destroying the equipment at random can kill people.
8. **Neither donor nor patient loses moral agency.** The donor can refuse continued participation. The patient can reject a proposed treatment. The player cannot decide that another person's life or body is an available resource merely because a preferred ending needs it.
9. **The method does not restore old losses.** This is essential to the investigation and its emotional meaning. A depiction of Octavian or a preserved impression can explain Armand's vulnerability; it cannot become a last-minute living son.
10. **The process is not a routine benefit without consequence.** Successful rescue exposes a powerful practice requiring oversight, resources, and consent. A sequel may examine those consequences; it cannot reset the city as if nothing was learned.

These are fictional metaphysical rules. They need readable demonstrations and limitations, not technical lectures in every scene. The ordinary patient and caregiving scenes should still be about the people involved.

### 8.6 Branches that change the story

- Reporting the first incident through ordinary channels can involve Gareth sooner, create a usable public record, and complicate covert access. Keeping it private changes those risks; it is not automatically wiser.
- Trusting Quentin early may protect him from punitive confinement while making verification harder. Pressing him too aggressively can close a personal relationship even if it yields a useful fact.
- Sharing evidence across communities changes who can corroborate it, who is exposed, and which resources become available. It also permits people to decide whether they want involvement.
- Exposing Orrell's old concealment can weaken the authority needed for an organized rescue while making future accountability possible. Negotiating temporary cooperation has its own cost.
- Confronting a peripheral accomplice can cause a traceable move of records or captives. Protecting surveillance may preserve access while extending immediate harm. The story should reveal enough for that choice to be meaningful.
- The distributed rescue is achievable through different coalitions. It requires corroborated knowledge, materials, capable collaborators, and willing adult participants. Failure to develop it leaves harder rescue options; it should never be hidden behind one obscure dialogue selection.
- Donors and patients participate in decisions about treatment. The protagonist's preferred romance cannot overrule Eamon's refusal or Quentin's informed choice.
- The final disclosure can distinguish criminal responsibility, institutional reform, patient privacy, and the wider question of supernatural secrecy. These are related decisions, not one all-or-nothing button.

An ending can solve the murders while leaving a relationship unresolved, save a relationship while ending a career in Calder, or achieve a collective rescue while losing faith in an institution. Avoid a single moral score that converts all those differences into one good or bad label.

## 9. The prebuilt novel: chapters, branches, endings, and epilogues

This is the proposed finite story architecture. The writers may refine it before the manuscript is locked. A published playthrough follows a fully written path through it; Claude never chooses the next incident by improvising what the world might do. The mystery's causes and perpetrators remain the same across canonical paths. Choices change discovery, action, relationships, cooperation, cost, accountability, and the protagonist's future.

### 9.1 Chapter structure and calendar

Use twenty-four chapter containers. Each contains complete authored scenes, some common and some conditional. Chapters can differ substantially in location and cast between paths, but each has a defined entry state, dramatic purpose, exit state, and next chapter. An authored transition can cover hours or days naturally. This calendar organizes causality; it does not require a date heading or summary passage at every transition.

| Chapter | Working time | Fixed dramatic purpose | Prebuilt variation and carried consequence |
|---|---|---|---|

The first three deaths and returns keep their established causes regardless of path. A player can change how much suffering is prevented afterward and what happens to each surviving person. If development later adds a branch that prevents a scheduled death, every dependent chapter, clue, relationship, portrait state, and ending must be written for that alternate history before shipping it.

### 9.2 Authored supporting-arc windows

Each S-arc receives four documented states: introduced; foregrounded or backgrounded; resolved; epilogue acknowledged. A backgrounded arc has a fixed or conditional author-written outcome. It is not simulated, endlessly pending, or narrated as if the protagonist attended an unplayed scene.

| Arcs | Main authored windows | Resolution requirement |
|---|---|---|
| S01 print shop; S13 Will and Isaac | CH01–02, CH07, CH16, CH23 | Establish the obligation, make the relevant choice visible, and write the household consequence. |
| S02 Nolan leaving; S06 venue lease | CH01, CH07, CH11–12, CH17, CH23 | Course and lease deadlines are fixed. Write staying, leaving, or changed employment without treating romance as automatic cancellation. |
| S03 Ellis's placement; S12 the film | CH05, CH07, CH11, CH16–17, CH23 | Resolve attribution, work, and applications using known choices. Account for Felix's fate in the film's completion. |
| S04 Serrano family; S08 Wesley's housing | CH07, CH12, CH17–18, CH23 | Private support and family expectations produce a concrete living/work arrangement. |
| S05 Dominic performing; S16 Regent life | CH04, CH11–12, CH17, CH23 | A performance and a residents' decision have written outcomes distinct from solving the murders. |
| S07 promotion; S09 response service | CH04, CH09–10, CH18–20, CH23 | Professional outcomes reflect testimony, competence, and cooperation rather than affection points. |
| S10 night work; S11 tenants | CH06–07, CH09, CH16, CH23 | Provide a practical outcome and a witnessable effect on people the protagonist knows. |
| S14 crossing succession | CH06, CH13–15, CH23–24 | Resolve or formally defer the agreement on explicit terms; an authored message can report a background resolution. |
| S15 bakery succession | CH08, CH16, CH22–24 | Silas's survival and his own decision govern the outcome. Never assign him a future job if he died. |

The partial haunting at the Lantern Rooms is a short authored scene in CH12's human-work branch. It has its own cause and resolution, even though that branch is shorter than a major adventure chapter. All six additional supernatural premises now have chapter assignments: Northline and the screen in CH09; the gathering, Regent emergency, and haunting in CH12; crossing closure in CH14.

### 9.3 Branch depth and convergence

Use three levels deliberately: expressive choices alter the narrator's attitude and written response; scene branches provide different substantial encounters or methods; structural branches change available plans, relationships, final outcomes, or epilogues. Record which kind each choice is. A cosmetic choice must not pretend to control survival, and a life-changing choice needs enough context for the player to understand its stakes.

Branching does not require a complete separate novel after every menu. Converge after a scene's consequences have been recorded and acknowledged. A return to CH10 can have different dialogue, allies, evidence provenance, and emotional context. It cannot silently restore a lost invitation or pretend an argument never occurred. CH13–15 and CH20–22 provide extended route variation with complete dramatic sequences.

Maintain a finite scene list and reserve every conditional scene's place before drafting prose. No repeatable favor loops, unlimited relationship grinding, freeform quest generation, or real-time background progression are required by this novel. Every canonical path reaches CH24 and an explicit end, including nonromantic and costly outcomes.

### 9.4 Six complete plot-ending families

These are author-facing names. The final game may reveal an ending title after its last scene, but the prose must carry the outcome. They are categories of resolved story, not a single good/bad score. Each requires its own CH22 dramatic sequence, CH23 aftermath variants, and CH24 base epilogue.

| Ending ID and working name | Established condition and final decision | Fixed core outcome |
|---|---|---|
| END_A — The Shared Return | A fully tested distributed bridge, willing participants, adequate materials, and institutional oversight have been secured; the player chooses accountable institutional cooperation. | All three patients survive and all three captive donors are freed. The operation closes. Its evidence supports an accountable response and a governed recovery program. Institutional wrongdoing is addressed explicitly. |
| END_B — A City of Witnesses | The distributed bridge is ready, but the player chooses an adequately prepared cross-community coalition with independent evidence custody. | All six survive; donors are freed; the operation closes. Communities establish their own recovery and accountability arrangements. The public disclosure level is separately chosen from authored options and never outs a romance. |
| END_C — The Long Recovery | The full distributed method is not ready or is declined, but a validated interim bridge and informed volunteer support for all three patients are ready. | Donors are freed; all patients survive with a longer recovery and concrete support commitments. The ring ends. The aftermath includes care, financial/work consequences, and a timetable rather than an unexplained cure. |
| END_D — The Private Settlement | A credible negotiated arrangement has been established in CH19, with enforceable evidence leverage, a validated interim bridge, and willing replacement support. | The three captives are freed and the patients survive. Damian's operation ends, while Armand retains specified influence and obtains limited protection from exposure. The protagonist knowingly accepts a compromise with lasting institutional and personal costs. No new captive silently replaces an old one. |
| END_E — The Severed Bond | The player explicitly chooses to free donors without an available or accepted continuing bridge after the cost is plainly established. | Eamon, Hugo, and Clive survive and regain autonomy; Quentin, Silas, and Felix die when their support is ended under the story's rules. The operation is stopped and the case resolved. The ending gives grief, accountability, and donor perspectives full scenes. It cannot show a surviving Quentin romance. |
| END_F — What We Could Save | Only the validated single-pair emergency bridge can be executed, or a clearly signaled CH21 disruption has reduced capacity to one; the player makes an informed patient-priority choice. | All three captive donors are freed. Exactly one selected patient survives; the other two die. Write three subvariants: END_F_Q for Quentin, END_F_S for Silas, END_F_F for Felix. Resolve the operation and the survivors' futures without implying all outcomes were equivalent. |

Do not choose an ending through a hidden morality total or an arbitrary last-minute affection check. CH18–20 explicitly establish which methods are ready and why. Preparation opens options; the player makes the final value-laden selection. When both END_A and END_B are available, the institutional choice determines which, not an undocumented tie-break. END_C and END_D differ through a real negotiated decision and consequences, even though their immediate survival totals match.

Keep all release paths resolvable. If no continuing bridge is ready, END_E remains a clearly explained path. END_F requires the actual single-pair technique to have been demonstrated; it never appears as a convenient invention. A compromised full plan can degrade only through prewritten CH21 events with visible causes and choices. A failure sequence must still resolve the mystery and continue to its written aftermath.

Damian's fate, Armand's exposure, August's accountability, and treatment of peripheral accomplices use a small documented set of chapter outcomes. Define each compatible tuple before drafting CH22. A private settlement cannot accidentally coexist with prose announcing that every benefactor has been publicly convicted. Canonical truth about responsibility never changes to match the selected route.

### 9.5 Epilogues are authored scenes

CH24 occurs one year after the rescue. Its narration remains first-person present. Letters, visits, shared work, conversation, and remembered events allow the protagonist to know what happened. Do not switch into an omniscient census of sixty strangers' futures.

Construct each epilogue in this fixed editorial order:

1. **The ending's anchor scene:** one of six written frames establishes where the protagonist is and what the resolution has changed.
2. **Patients and donors:** select the compatible survival/recovery passages. No person is forgotten merely because they were not a romance option.
3. **The primary relationship or chosen single life:** use a fully written route ending, including a satisfying nonromantic alternative.
4. **Two or three supporting consequences:** draw from arcs actually known and developed on that path. Unmet characters do not suddenly receive intimate biographies.
5. **The final scene or image:** close the protagonist's central emotional movement. It may suggest continued life, but this book's conflict and chosen relationship receive a conclusion.

All component prose and every necessary connective passage must be written and edited in advance. A selector combines compatible passages; a live model does not invent careers, deaths, partners, children, reconciliations, or sequel hooks. Review representative complete combinations for rhythm so the result reads like one ending rather than a report assembled from cards.

| Relationship route | Authored private together ending | Authored future apart / unresolved ending | Nonromantic or loss handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adrian | A private visit after his shift shows how duty and choosing time together now coexist. | A transfer or damaged trust receives a specific departure or reunion scene, according to the route decision. | A professional friendship has its own emotional payoff. |
| Micah | A home or shared routine reflects his negotiated independence; the protagonist has not simply replaced family demands. | Distance or incompatible needs is discussed honestly in the written route ending. | The friendship acknowledges his chosen work and family boundaries. |
| Ellis | A visit around his placement shows a deliberately maintained private relationship, nearby or long-distance as chosen. | The placement proceeds; the separation is a consequence of the actual relationship decisions. | A friendship scene preserves his ambition and the protagonist's support or estrangement. |
| Dominic | A performance and a private moment afterward show a life he has reclaimed. | A written separation respects his autonomy and the history between them. | His music and family arc conclude independently of romance. |
| Nolan | Staying or long-distance is explicitly agreed; his training remains part of his future. | A departure scene allows affection without promising a relationship neither chose. | The old friendship can endure, change, or end through authored consequences. |
| Ansel | Visits across the established crossings show a relationship he chooses for himself. | Duty, trust, or incompatible plans produce a written parting rather than an unexplained disappearance. | His independence and the crossing dispute receive a nonromantic payoff. |
| Quentin | Only if alive: an ordinary day after recovery makes the relationship about the life he chooses. | Only if alive: a written decision about intimacy or distance respects what both men want. | If dead under END_E or END_F_S/F, use a grief epilogue. Never display his living portrait or romance scene. |
| Reuben | A reciprocal domestic or leisure scene shows that care and needing care can coexist. | A boundary or career disagreement leads to its prewritten resolution. | His service and friendships receive a full nonromantic outcome. |

During detailed route outlining, write at least three romantic terminal scenes per man: private together, an agreed future involving distance or changed circumstances, and separation after meaningful involvement. Each route also has a friendship/not-pursued treatment. Quentin additionally needs his conditional loss scene. These are production requirements, not claims that the full scenes already exist in this planning document.

Epilogue compatibility checks must enforce survival, relationship status, career outcome, location, public/private boundaries, protagonist knowledge, and the one-year date. A future portrait or artwork is selected by the same resolved facts as the prose.


## 10. Romance, choices, and private discovery

### Separate the variables that fiction needs

Track familiarity, trust, attraction, comfort with intimacy, reliability, unresolved hurt, and public/private boundaries separately. They need not be numbers exposed to the reader. High attraction with low trust produces a different story from affectionate friendship or a relationship strained by incompatible plans. Being skilled at investigation does not make every man desire the protagonist.

The protagonist's own inner life is selected through prewritten multiple-choice options. A moment can offer attention, avoidance, desire, irritation, curiosity, uncertainty, or an interpretation outside romance, depending on context. The surrounding first-person prose must honor the chosen interpretation. Do not present four differently worded versions of the same attraction and call that agency.

### Distinct paths of recognition

| Route | What he initially calls the attachment | Evidence that challenges his explanation | An earned next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adrian | Responsibility or professional concern | He seeks the protagonist after duty is finished and cannot explain why disagreement hurts. | A private admission of wanting contact, followed by respect for the protagonist's answer. |
| Micah | Friendship and shared enjoyment | He begins wanting time alone and feels unsettled by his own awareness of another man's body. | Naming a specific desire without being required to announce a complete identity. |
| Ellis | Interest, aesthetics, a person worth knowing | Being impressive stops satisfying him; he wants to be wanted on an ordinary bad day. | Letting a private uncertainty remain visible and returning to the conversation. |
| Dominic | Gratitude for being treated normally | He wants intimacy that involves his changed body and his unchanged preferences. | Asking what another man wants, rather than arranging his own withdrawal on that man's behalf. |
| Nolan | Longstanding friendship | Familiar gestures begin to feel newly charged and he cannot dismiss them as habit. | A direct, awkward conversation that allows either a relationship or a valued friendship. |
| Ansel | Duty, hospitality, useful company | He invents official reasons for visits and resents the invention. | Coming without a pretext and keeping a confidence without turning it into an obligation. |
| Quentin | Trust in a witness and practical alliance | He wants the protagonist's company on a day when nothing needs investigating. | Initiating time together as a person with choices, after room has been made for those choices. |
| Reuben | Concern for someone's welfare | He wants to be cared for and finds professional competence an inadequate shield. | Accepting help or asking for closeness outside a caregiving role. |

These are authoring directions for the eight route packets. Before release, assign their recognition, hesitation, conflict, and resolution beats to specific scene IDs and write every supported variation. Recognition can be uneven within that authored design. The protagonist can notice attraction before the other man does, but reciprocation follows the prewritten route conditions rather than a live model's guess.

### Beyond a first kiss

Private relationships have practical life: messages, uneven schedules, being invited somewhere, arguments, affection, boundaries, sexual discovery where appropriate to the chosen tone, and decisions about the future. Neither desire nor a supernatural species makes someone effortlessly experienced. Intimacy should reveal specific people and develop through mutual willingness. A private relationship may remain private at the ending.

An unchosen route still has an arc. Adrian can become a better warden; Micah can leave home; Ellis can take the placement; Dominic can perform; Nolan can move; Ansel can establish independence; Quentin can pursue training; Reuben can build his service. Other relationships may develop with time, but no one is automatically reassigned into a public gay couple as a consolation ending.

## 11. Authored scene and branch contract

### The manuscript is complete content

Classic must be able to play every route, ending, and epilogue from stored authored content with live AI disabled. That includes transitions, failed attempts, declined invitations, nonromantic alternatives, scene variants for earlier choices, and the final aftermath. A promise that Claude will fill a gap later does not satisfy this requirement.

Each scene has a stable ID, chapter position, entry conditions, time and place, cast, allowed knowledge, canonical text, choice list, state changes, next-scene destinations, and art references. A prose-only emotional choice can still alter an authored response or future interpretation. Its limited scope should remain honest.

| Scene field | Required authoring decision |
|---|---|
| Identity and chronology | Unique scene ID, chapter, time window, previous scene eligibility, and allowed next scenes. |
| Narrative function | What changes in this scene and why it belongs at this point in the novel. |
| Canonical content | Complete first-person-present prose, including relevant conditional paragraphs. |
| Cast and knowledge | Who is physically present, what each knows, and which facts the narrator may disclose. |
| Choices | Stable option IDs, exact text, availability conditions, and whether the effect is expressive, relational, investigative, or structural. |
| Consequences | Explicit variables changed by each option; promises, evidence, injuries, privacy, time, and relationship interpretation as applicable. |
| Convergence | What prior differences must be acknowledged when branches rejoin. |
| Art | Background ID, portrait identity/expression/outfit, optional scene illustration, and a neutral fallback where needed. |
| Completion | Where every option goes. A terminal scene specifies the ending or epilogue it completes. |

### A manageable authored state model

Use finite, documented values. Avoid a universal goodness meter or an opaque relationship score that silently decides whether a man is attracted. Suggested state groups are:

- `evidence`: which E-clues are known, their sources, whether corroborated, and who has been told.
- `relationships`: acquaintance/friendship/romance stage, relevant trust conditions, explicit acceptance or refusal, unresolved conflict, and private disclosure boundaries for each route.
- `commitments`: promises made and their written outcomes; selected S-arcs; application, work, family, and crossing decisions.
- `rescue_readiness`: demonstrated full, interim, or single-pair method; required materials; eligible and informed collaborators; authorized access; established enemy awareness.
- `resolution`: the chosen ending ID, patient and donor survival, culprit outcomes, accountability choices, career/home outcomes, and primary relationship status.
- `presentation`: already introduced portraits, known identities, scene-specific injuries/outfits, and unlocked illustrations. Presentation reads the story state; it cannot change it.

Define legal combinations before implementation. A refused relationship can remain a friendship. An unseen clue cannot be used in dialogue until an authored scene supplies it. A future image cannot announce a career the player did not choose. Resolve state effects through the existing game's supported structure after inspecting it; these names are a content specification, not an assertion that those exact fields already exist in the app.

### Example scene specification

`CH05.HOSPITAL.01` takes place on Day 6 at P23 with Reuben, Rafi, and Ilyas in their established roles. It is entered after the player accepts the hospital investigation path. Quentin participates in the agreed tests on authored terms. The scene establishes E05: there is an external source supporting him. It cannot yet identify Eamon, Damian, or the holding site.

The menu offers three prewritten emphases: explain the protagonist's unusual perception to Reuben; ask Ilyas for an independently recorded account; or focus the conversation on what Quentin has been told and who will receive the findings. Each receives its own dialogue and relationship consequence. All successful variants retain the physical finding and its actual provenance. Refusal or delay has a written route to a later corroboration opportunity. The scene then moves to one of its specified successors. It does not ask a live model to decide what a new medical test discovers.

### Canon-preserving AI retelling

If the existing Varied mode uses Claude, give the model a scene packet: the selected canonical scene, relevant prior facts, public character profiles, current voice and relationship stage, player-selected inner interpretation, permitted description, exact choice IDs, and the immutable result. Keep unrevealed author-only spoilers outside the packet unless they are strictly necessary for that scene.

Allowed changes include phrasing, sentence rhythm, transitions within the same beat, and description consistent with the approved scene. Preserve the order of causally important events and any exact clue wording or testimony the puzzle needs. A retelling cannot create a new clue, suspicion presented as fact, attraction, confession, power, named character, scene exit, injury, time jump, or ending. It cannot turn a tentative interpretation selected by the player into certainty.

Choices and state effects come from the authored graph. Ideally render the exact authored option text; if the existing app already supports option paraphrases, preserve the option IDs and prove that meanings remain equivalent. Generated prose never directly awards evidence or chooses a next node. Invalid or unavailable retelling falls back to the complete canonical text. This describes desired behavior to integrate with existing modes, not a request to build a new mode system.

### Journal and interface content

Any journal records only discovered people, places, evidence, and commitments. It cannot label an unrevealed culprit, romance route, closeted man, or supernatural identity. An optional scene illustration reinforces an authored moment; its absence must not make a clue inaccessible. A player should be able to understand and finish the novel through the prose and choices alone.

Letters, messages, and portraits are written or selected for specified scenes. Paper letters have an in-story reason. Existing contemporary communication remains available where established; the plot must account for it. Neither a generated letter nor a portrait caption can introduce an unsupported fact.


## 12. Writing the breadth on the page

During manuscript drafting, close attention belongs to what matters in the selected scene. A crowded meal can turn on one person's hesitation. A journey can occupy one sentence when it changes nothing, or several scenes when the travel itself changes relationships. The amount of narrative space follows consequence, interest, and emotional pressure. There is no alternating template.

Use recurring places differently. P20 first appears as a shop, later as a difficult workplace, then as a family kitchen, then as somewhere the protagonist comes simply to see Ellis. P13 can be an employer, a crime scene, a rehearsal space, a celebration, and a threatened community resource. These uses should accumulate memory rather than reset the setting on each visit.

Planned new settings are introduced through people in the authored chapter sequence. An invitation introduces a house; an errand leads into another district; a friend's work reveals an unfamiliar part of the city; a case reaches someone who knows a route outside it. A place does not need a supernatural secret to deserve a scene. Conversely, a major discovery deserves enough space for the protagonist and reader to understand what has changed.

Keep dialogue differentiated. Adrian asks for a sequence; Micah tells a practical story; Ellis qualifies; Dominic avoids solemnity; Nolan notices an absurd detail; Ansel measures an obligation; Quentin demands a usable next step; Reuben waits for an answer. These tendencies vary with stress and intimacy. They are not catchphrases or reasons to make everyone sound clipped.

Let the city be enjoyable. Competence, teasing, good food, bad films, successful work, earned rest, physical attraction, a shared view, and private affection are substantial pleasures. The supernatural world should offer fascination and possibility as well as danger. Mature themes do not require continuous misery or graphic treatment.

## 13. Production lock and validation

### Authoring freedom and published canon

Trent and Claude can revise the book during development. Once a story version is released, its world facts, mystery truth, cast, visual identities, scene graph, choices, and outcomes are fixed for that version. Player freedom consists of its authored choices; model variation presents the selected story consistently. The existing Full AI Freedom mode remains a separate app capability.

The private-romance constraint, gay male protagonist, first-person present prose, large ensemble, broad city, and natural narrative movement remain governing creative requirements. All current names, places, abilities, and the proposed 24-chapter architecture may be coherently revised by the writers before lock. They are not imported facts from earlier installments, which have not been supplied here.

### Production sequence for a complete book

1. **Resolve the planning draft.** Select the exact chapter branches, route scope, customization options, crime chronology, and ending conditions. Decide which location entries appear in full scenes and which remain background. Remove unused promises or assign them a real payoff.
2. **Create the finite scene inventory.** Give every scene and option an ID. Define entry and exit conditions, event order, knowledge, state changes, and art needs. Every side arc gets foreground and background outcomes.
3. **Write one full canonical path to a finished epilogue.** Use it to establish narrative voice, chapter length, transitions, and the experience of a complete novel. This is a vertical production slice, not the final release.
4. **Write all alternate branches and route material.** Complete eight relationship paths, friendship/single alternatives, six plot-ending families including END_F's three survival variants, and compatible epilogues. No release path may depend on an unwritten promise or live improvisation.
5. **Edit complete playthroughs.** Review scenes in the order a reader encounters them, including alternative locations and relationship states. Check that clues are fair, emotional changes earned, and recurring settings acquire memory.
6. **Integrate art against the locked scene inventory.** Establish portrait identities before mass-producing expressions. Reuse successful environment work through deliberate scene assignments. All visible assets must be compatible with story facts.
7. **Validate the canonical experience and optional retelling.** Test the authored edition to completion, then check whether any existing Varied integration preserves it. Do not treat an attractive opening or a working API call as evidence that all endings work.

Estimate total prose and art after the scene inventory. The intended experience is a full novel with substantial route variation. Avoid compressing relationships into brief summaries to meet an arbitrary small prototype target; also avoid padding every supporting character to identical prominence.

### Required checks for the published manuscript

| Check | What passing means |
|---|---|
| Reachability | Every declared ending and epilogue type has at least one documented legal path; every nonterminal choice has a successor. |
| Completion | Every reachable scene has canonical prose, options, transitions, and valid art/fallback references. No placeholder is solved through live invention. |
| Causality | Date, travel, injuries, return windows, evidence provenance, and off-screen events remain consistent on each route. |
| Knowledge | Dialogue, choices, journal entries, and portraits disclose only what the protagonist can know. |
| Consequence | Branch convergence preserves relevant decisions and resolves or deliberately closes introduced commitments. |
| Endings | Patient/donor survival, culprit outcomes, institutional decisions, and epilogue scenes agree. END_F resolves exactly the selected patient's survival. |
| Romance | All eight routes and nonromantic play are complete; refusals, boundaries, private discovery, and relationship endings are honored. No essential evidence requires romance. |
| Art continuity | Portraits preserve identity, age, outfit, expression, injury, and survival. An alternate scene never displays an unvisited or destroyed location by mistake. |
| Optional retelling | The same choice sequence yields the same canonical facts and outcome across Classic and a canon-preserving Varied run. Wording differences cannot alter the game state. |

Exercise at least one full path to each of END_A–END_E and END_F_Q/S/F, each relationship's terminal categories, and a deliberately nonromantic path. Add targeted combinations where facts interact: Quentin's romance with each survival outcome, a character leaving Calder, rejected institutional cooperation, and a closed supporting arc. Exhaustive enumeration of every cosmetic choice is unnecessary; targeted coverage must address actual conditional risks.

### Status of this revision

## 15. Pixel-art direction and portrait workflow

### 15.1 Preserve what already works

Trent reports that Claude's environments are strong and its faces have been inconsistent. No existing screenshots, assets, or drawing code were provided for this revision, so the precise failure is unconfirmed. This plan addresses common structural problems and gives Claude a repeatable way to establish identities. It does not claim to have generated, inspected, or repaired the app's actual portraits.

Begin by examining the app's existing successful environment assets and recording their native resolution, palette behavior, outline treatment, light direction, detail density, camera perspective, and display scaling. Use those as the project's visual reference. Preserve working environment production rather than restarting it to match a newly invented style. Portraits should share its lighting and degree of stylization while remaining readable at their actual display size.

The target is expressive, attractive, recognizably adult male characters with individual faces. Avoid a single handsome head recolored fifty-eight times, chibi proportions, decorative facial scars, permanently seductive expressions, or an identical sharp jaw on every man. Cast design comes from each person's age, body, work, history, and chosen appearance. A name alone does not determine ethnicity, skin tone, facial structure, or manner.

### 15.2 Working asset sizes

These are proposed defaults only if the existing app lacks a suitable established specification. Confirm its current viewport and source assets before adopting them. Native resolution means the actual pixel drawing, not a larger export containing blocks of duplicated pixels.

| Asset | Proposed native size | Use and production rule |
|---|---|---|
| Main bust portrait | 128 × 160 px, transparent background | Head and upper torso, consistent eye-level camera and three-quarter pose. Keep enough facial pixels to show differences in cheek, nose, eye, and mouth structure. |
| Larger portrait alternative | 160 × 192 px | Use only if the three-face pilot remains unreadable at the intended display size. Make one project-level resolution decision before batching. |
| Environment | Existing approved app size; 320 × 180 px is a fallback starting point | Preserve successful perspective, palette, and framing. Different views of one place receive distinct view IDs. |
| Narrative snapshot / scene illustration | Same scene aspect ratio as existing app; 320 × 180 px fallback | Compose an authored moment. Use approved characters and scene-state variants. |
| Small journal portrait | 48 × 48 or 64 × 64 px if the interface needs it | Make a deliberately simplified crop or redraw. Do not assume an automatically reduced bust keeps its eyes and mouth readable. |

Display with nearest-neighbor scaling where the renderer supports it. Use integer enlargement when the layout permits it, preserving aspect ratio; check the actual mobile layout rather than stretching a portrait to fill any box. Keep original native-size exports as well as any enlarged previews. Do not save repeatedly resized copies as the new master.

Use editable source layers and lossless PNG exports with transparent portrait backgrounds. Avoid smoothing, JPEG artifacts, and accidental semitransparent edge halos. These are project production choices. An editor such as Aseprite can support reference images, layers, and nearest-neighbor resizing; the existing art pipeline can also satisfy the plan without changing tools.

### 15.3 The face pilot: three characters first

Produce only **Adrian C01, Micah C02, and Ellis C03** as the initial neutral portraits. They offer three different silhouettes, face widths, hair structures, and expressions of adulthood. Use the existing approved environment style as a reference beside them. Do not create the other fifty-five faces until these three establish a convincing visual approach.

For each pilot:

1. **Write the identity sheet.** Specify head shape, proportions, complexion and undertone, hairline and texture, brow, eye spacing and shape, nose, mouth, ears, facial hair if any, neck, build, clothing, and two or three distinguishing features. Preserve the written character's age and build. Select a single reference direction; do not change the person between attempts.
2. **Sketch the structure.** Use a simple reference drawing or reference image to resolve the head's three-dimensional form and three-quarter perspective. A high-resolution reference can guide the pixel drawing; shrinking it alone does not finish pixel art.
3. **Draw silhouette and major planes.** Establish skull, jaw, neck, and shoulders with a small set of coherent value groups. Inspect this before adding eyelashes, iris highlights, beard texture, or tiny accessories.
4. **Place facial landmarks deliberately.** Draw a construction guide for this individual. Keep the far eye, brow, cheek, nostril, and mouth consistent with the pose. Individual asymmetry is useful; accidental perspective mismatch is not.
5. **Build clean pixel clusters.** Shape eyelids, nose planes, lips, and cheek transitions with connected intentional forms. Avoid isolated noise pixels and outlining every facial feature with the darkest color.
6. **Apply the skin ramp and hair shapes.** Start with approximately five to seven skin values plus a few useful hue variations, then adjust for the established art style. Give different complexions suitable highlights and shadows rather than recoloring the same ramp indiscriminately. Hair reads first as large masses and a distinct hairline.
7. **Inspect at native size and the actual game display size.** Also view an enlarged nearest-neighbor preview to repair stray pixels. A portrait that only looks good at 800% is unfinished.
8. **Compare the three pilots together.** Remove names and temporarily simplify hair/clothing during review. Distinctive underlying faces should remain. Select and document the strongest consistent approach, then revise the weaker portraits to match its craft, not its anatomy.

This is an art review gate, not a request to stop after every sketch. Claude can iterate and select the strongest internal draft. Trent can redirect the look if desired; the production plan does not require repeated permission for routine pixel corrections.

### 15.4 Construction guidance for the 128 × 160 portrait

For an initial bust layout, reserve roughly the top two-thirds for the head and neck, with shoulders continuing into the lower frame. A head about 45–65 pixels wide usually leaves enough room for distinct features in this proposed canvas. These are layout starting points to adjust to the individual, not one mandatory face template.

Establish the center plane, brow line, nose base, mouth line, and chin before rendering. A three-quarter face requires a narrower far side; placing two identical front-facing eyes onto it creates an incoherent head. Keep highlights subordinate to eyelids so eyes do not become bright marbles. Let the nose emerge from its planes and shadow rather than a generic dark triangle. Use a restrained mouth cluster with a lower-lip light only when it helps. An expression should not depend entirely on adding a red blush or enlarging the eyes.

At this size, a few pixels can change apparent age, gaze, and emotion. Keep jaw and cheek structure adult without making every young man bearded or angular. Keep teeth as a restrained grouped shape when visible. Avoid drawing individual bright teeth, symmetrical white eye rings, an unbroken black mouth slit, or random single-pixel stubble across the whole face.

If Claude draws programmatically, code is the drawing medium. Author each face's landmark coordinates and pixel shapes deliberately; do not rely on a universal oval plus interchangeable eyes, hair, and mouth. Shared helpers can manage palettes, layers, export, or scale. They must not erase identity by forcing every man into identical geometry. Keep the approved face mask and feature layers as stable source data.

### 15.6 Expressions come from the approved master

Once a neutral portrait is accepted, retain its exact skull, hairline, nose structure, ears, palette identity, and anchor position. Duplicate the layered master and edit expression-relevant parts. Do not generate each expression as an unrelated new portrait and hope the same person appears.

For each main route, plan six baseline expressions: **neutral, attentive, amused, tense, hurt, and guarded warmth**. These are visual states selected for a written scene. Guarded warmth is only used after an earned beat; it must not reveal a secret attraction before the prose and choices permit it.

Expressions draw on brow angle, lid shape, gaze, cheek compression, and mouth corners together. Keep the head size and face placement steady between frames. A large head turn needs a separately constructed approved pose rather than a warped neutral image. Hair, scars, freckles, and clothing should not flicker between expressions. An outfit change uses a clothing layer or its own approved master, preserving the identity beneath it.

Begin with still portraits. Add blinking or breathing only after identity and expressions work. If animation is used, keep a small authored loop, avoid moving the entire face like a rubber mask, and provide a still alternative. No animation is necessary to make a strong narrative portrait.

### 15.7 Face repair guide

| Symptom | Likely issue to inspect | Targeted correction |
|---|---|---|
| Characters look like the same man | Shared skull, jaw, eye spacing, and nose despite changed hair. | Revise underlying geometry and proportions before adding accessories. |
| Eyes look empty or startled | Excessive white, floating pupils, identical eye openings, weak eyelids. | Rebuild the lid and socket clusters, then add restrained iris/highlight pixels. |
| Face reads as a child | Oversized eyes, abbreviated lower face, tiny nose, undersized neck. | Adjust the adult structural proportions while retaining that character's age and softness. |
| Mouth looks pasted on | Uniform dark line, lip colors disconnected from skin, no cheek response. | Rebuild the mouth's volume and neighboring planes as one expression. |
| Smile changes the person | Jaw, nose, hairline, or eye placement rebuilt with each variant. | Return to the approved master; edit only the necessary muscles and feature layers. |
| Skin looks dirty | Noise, indiscriminate dithering, too many unrelated values, random stubble. | Simplify into coherent plane clusters and a controlled ramp. |
| Portrait looks soft in the app | Smoothing, fractional stretching, or repeated resizing. | Inspect export and rendering separately; use the native master and supported nearest-neighbor display. |
| Dark skin becomes unreadable at night | Lighting applied as a broad black overlay that collapses the facial values. | Preserve value separation and use an authored night-light palette or controlled lighting layer. |
| Attractive in close-up, weak at game size | Detail is carrying identity that the display cannot resolve. | Strengthen silhouette, planes, brows, nose, and mouth; simplify fine texture. |

Review one neutral and one subtle expression in the actual interface before mass export. These are recommended diagnostic checks, not claims about which particular errors occurred in Claude's previous work.

### 15.8 Optional editor references

The production choices above are specific to this proposed art direction. For teams already using Aseprite, its official documentation describes [reference layers](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/reference-layer/), the [layer/frame/cel model and editable source files](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/basics/), and [nearest-neighbor resizing](https://aseprite.org/api/command/SpriteSize). No particular editor purchase or API integration is required by this bible.
